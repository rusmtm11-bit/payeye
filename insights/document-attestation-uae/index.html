Document attestation in the UAE: a practical checklist.

Attestation is the process of officially verifying that a document is genuine so it can be used in the UAE. It trips up a lot of businesses because it's sequential — each step depends on the one before — and a single skipped stamp can send you back to the start.

What usually needs attestation

  • Educational certificates for employment visas
  • Commercial documents and powers of attorney
  • Marriage and birth certificates for dependants' visas
  • Company documents issued abroad

The usual order of steps

For a document issued outside the UAE, the sequence typically runs:

  1. Attestation in the country of origin (notary, then relevant ministry)
  2. Attestation by that country's Ministry of Foreign Affairs
  3. Attestation by the UAE embassy in that country
  4. Final attestation by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs
  5. Legal translation into Arabic where required

Documents issued inside the UAE follow a shorter path, but the principle is the same: verify at each level before moving up.

Where the delays actually come from

Rarely the stamps themselves — usually the preparation. The most common causes are a document that wasn't attested in the origin country first, a translation done before the final attestation (so it has to be redone), or missing supporting copies. Sequence discipline is everything.

Attestation rewards patience and order. Rushing a step almost always costs more time than it saves.
