Setting up HR admin records for a UAE free-zone company.

Hiring your first employees in a free zone is exciting. The paperwork behind it is not — but getting it right early saves a great deal of pain later, especially when a licence renewal or an audit arrives. Here's the practical foundation.

Start with the entity, not the employee

Before a single contract is signed, make sure the company's own records are current: the trade licence, establishment card and any authority-specific registrations. Employee files sit on top of these; if the foundation is out of date, everything above it is too.

The core employee file

For each hire, keep a single, consistent file containing:

  • Signed employment contract in the authority's format
  • Passport, visa and Emirates ID copies, with expiry dates tracked
  • Educational and professional certificates, attested where required
  • Offer letter, job description and any addenda
  • Bank details for payroll and the WPS record

Track the dates, not just the documents

The single most common failure is a lapsed visa or licence nobody was watching. Maintain one renewal calendar covering visas, Emirates IDs, the establishment card and the licence, with reminders well ahead of each deadline. A document is only useful if it's valid.

Attendance and leave from day one

Decide how attendance, leave and timesheets are recorded before the first employee starts, and apply it uniformly. Retrofitting a system across a team that's already working is far harder than starting clean.

Keep it MOHRE-aligned

Even free-zone entities benefit from records structured the way the wider UAE labour framework expects — contracts, end-of-service calculations and leave entitlements documented clearly. It makes any future transition or audit dramatically simpler.

Clean records aren't bureaucracy for its own sake. They're what lets you grow without tripping over your own history.
